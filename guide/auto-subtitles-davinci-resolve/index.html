How to Add Automatic (AI) Subtitles in DaVinci Resolve

By Antonio Pennino · Updated July 2026 · 6 min read

Short answer: To add automatic subtitles in DaVinci Resolve you have three options. DaVinci Resolve Studio 18.5+ has a built-in Create Subtitles from Audio tool. On the free version there's no native auto-subtitle feature, so you either place captions manually with Fusion Text+ or use a plugin like SmartSubs Pro that transcribes your audio locally with Whisper AI and builds word-by-word captions in both Free and Studio. Below is exactly how each method works, and when to pick which.

Method 1, the built-in tool (Studio only)

Since version 18.5, DaVinci Resolve Studio can transcribe audio into a subtitle track automatically. Here's the workflow:

  1. Open the Edit page and select your timeline.
  2. Go to the Subtitle track header, right-click, and choose Create Subtitles from Audio.
  3. Pick the spoken language and caption length, then click Create.
  4. Resolve generates a subtitle track you can edit line by line.

The catch: this feature is Studio only (the paid $295 version), it produces one static line at a time, no word-by-word animation, and styling options are limited to the basic subtitle inspector. It also can't translate.

Method 2, manual captions with Fusion Text+ (Free)

On the free version you can build fully animated captions with a Fusion Text+ node, but you have to place every word by hand: transcribe your audio, cut a Text+ clip for each word or phrase, and line each one up to the exact frame it's spoken. It gives you total creative control, but for a 60-second reel that can mean hundreds of manual placements, slow and error-prone.

Method 3, automatic word-by-word captions with SmartSubs Pro (Free & Studio)

SmartSubs Pro combines the automation of the built-in tool with the styling freedom of Text+, and it works on both Free and Studio. The workflow:

  1. Transcribe, Whisper AI runs locally and places every word on the exact frame it's spoken (99% accuracy, 90+ languages, 100% offline).
  2. Translate (optional), auto-translate the captions into 90+ languages while keeping the original timing.
  3. Emphasize (optional), Gemini listens to your voice and highlights the words you actually stress, karaoke-style.
  4. Style, point it at your own animated Text+ template and it drops every word onto the timeline in your font and animation.

Because it uses standard Fusion Text+ nodes, it runs on the free version too, and because transcription is local, your footage and audio never leave your machine.
