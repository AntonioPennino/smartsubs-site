SmartSubs Pro vs the Alternatives

By Antonio Pennino · Updated September 2026 · 7 min read

Straight up: there are good subtitle tools for DaVinci Resolve, and some of them are free. I built SmartSubs Pro, so I am not neutral, but I would rather you buy the right tool than the wrong one. Below is a fair look at the real options, AutoSubs, Magic Subtitles, the native Studio feature, and SmartSubs Pro, including where the free ones win and when you should not pay me anything.

AutoSubs, the best free option

AutoSubs by Tom Moroney is free and open source. It transcribes your timeline locally with Whisper, the same engine family SmartSubs uses, supports many languages and speaker detection, has a subtitle editor, and lets you style the output. It runs on both free and Studio Resolve.

Pick it when: you want a solid, private subtitle track without spending anything. It is genuinely good, and for a lot of people it is all they need.

Magic Subtitles, animated styles from Resolve's engine

Magic Subtitles by MrAlexTech generates animated subtitles on your timeline. It builds its script from Resolve's own transcription engine and feeds keyframes into a Text+ node, works offline, and comes as a free Lite tier plus a paid Pro. It is aimed at short, punchy content.

Pick it when: you like its ready-made animated look and want something that leans on Resolve's built-in transcription.

The native Studio feature

DaVinci Resolve Studio has Create Subtitles from Audio built in. It is reliable for a standard subtitle track, but it is Studio only, produces one static line at a time, and does no animation, no word-by-word, and no translation into other languages.

Pick it when: you already own Studio and only need plain, broadcast-style subtitles.

Where SmartSubs Pro genuinely differs

SmartSubs Pro is not trying to be the cheapest, it is built for one specific job: turning your voice into animated word-by-word captions on a look you designed. The things that actually set it apart:

  • Your own Text+ template. Instead of a fixed style, it drives a Fusion Text+ template you built, word by word, so the captions are yours, not a preset everyone recognizes.
  • AI emphasis from your actual voice. An optional Gemini step listens to pitch, volume and pacing to highlight the words you really stressed, not just keywords guessed from text.
  • Its own bundled Whisper engine. It does not depend on Resolve's transcription, so it runs on free Resolve at any version, all locally and offline.
  • Direct support from a working editor. When you email support, you reach me, Antonio, a video editor who uses this on real projects, usually within a day on weekdays.
  • One-time price. $39.99 once, no subscription.

When not to buy SmartSubs Pro

I would rather say this than have you refund later. Do not buy it if you only need a plain subtitle track, AutoSubs does that for free and does it well. Do not buy it if you are happy with Magic Subtitles' ready-made look. SmartSubs earns its price only when you want the captions to follow your own animated design, with emphasis pulled from how you actually spoke, and a person on the other end of support.
